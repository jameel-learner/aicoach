	# ===== RELATIONSHIP TO AI =====
	5.5.1. 
	- id: ai_as_collaborator
  		label: Treats AI as a collaborator, not an oracle or a tool
  		evidence: McKinsey State of Organizations 2026; GWI 2026 (Gen Z leads all cohorts on AI for skill-building, 40%)
  		implication: >
    	Expects to interrogate, contradict and redirect the tutor. A one-way explainer wastes the main affordance. Show reasoning; accept challenge.
  		features: [socratic_explainer, teach_it_back, challenge_mode, show_working]

	5.5.2. 
	- id: utility_seeking
  		label: Optimises for fast, useful answers
  		evidence: Razorfish/GWI Gen Alpha study (ChatGPT ranked 4th preferred learning medium, just below teachers); hacks/how-tos/DIY among top content genres
  		implication: >
    		Answer first, then unpack. Preamble, onboarding tours and "let me explain my approach" lose the session. Depth must be opt-in and one tap away.
  		features: [answer_first_layered_depth, hint_ladder, one_tap_resume]

	5.5.3. 
	- id: verification_reflex
  		label: Trusts only what it can check; verifies independently
  		evidence: ~70% of Gen Z trust a brand only after own research; ~49% independently verify online information; Gen Alpha measurably more brand-skeptical than parents at same age
  		implication: >
    		Highest-leverage trust feature in the whole app: cite the prescribed textbook location for every claim (board, chapter, page, exercise). An ungrounded AI tutor is assumed to be hallucinating, correctly. Admit uncertainty explicitly.
  		features: [textbook_citation_grounding, uncertainty_flagging, syllabus_scope_badge]

	5.5.4. 
	- id: anti_manipulation_detection
  		label: Detects and resents dark patterns
  		evidence: Gen Alpha low trust in TV personalities, 21% see celebrities as role models; Gen Z authenticity demand across sources
  		implication: >
    		Manufactured urgency, fake scarcity and loss-framed streak panic are detected and cost trust permanently. Every retention mechanic must survive the learner reading its source code.
  		features: [honest_progress_model, no_loss_framing]

	# ===== ATTENTION SHAPE =====
	5.5.5. 
	- id: short_session_high_intensity
  		label: Short sessions, high engagement within them — not short attention spans
  		evidence: Gen Alpha research consistently rejects the short-attention-span framing; sustained engagement in gaming contexts
  		implication: >
    		Design 7–12 minute units that are COMPLETE: each ends at a natural stopping point with a visible win. Never leave a learner mid-concept at session end.
  		features: [session_unit_7_12, natural_stopping_points, micro_win_close]

	5.5.6. 
	- id: mobile_video_first
  		label: Phone-first, video-first, vertical
		evidence: GWI 2026 (60%+ of Gen Z stream on phones; 60% used YouTube for video last month)
  		implication: >
    		Vertical layout, thumb-reachable controls, captions always on (shared-room and low-data use), works on mid-range Android over patchy mobile data.
  		features: [vertical_first_ui, captioned_microvideo, offline_pack, low_bandwidth_mode]

	5.5.7. 
	- id: emotional_bandwidth_management
  		label: Actively manages emotional load; names it early
  		evidence: GWI 2026 Gen Alpha analysis; Deloitte 2026 on financial/career pressure
  		implication: >
    		Board-exam stress is the dominant emotional context. Offer scope reduction ("just do 3 questions today") rather than cheerleading. Route distress to the existing Handoff disposition, never to a motivational message.
  		features: [reduce_scope_path, overwhelm_affordance, safety_handoff_routing]

	# ===== SOCIAL & IDENTITY =====
	5.5.8. 
	- id: gaming_native
  		label: Game literacy exceeds social-media literacy
  		evidence: Newzoo 2024 Global Gamer Study — Gen Alpha first generation to spend more time gaming than on social media; 53% want games/apps that teach useful skills
  		implication: >
    		They read game grammar fluently: levels, hint ladders, boss encounters, retry-without-shame, co-op. Borrow the GRAMMAR (mastery gating, instant feedback, failure as information) — not the MONETISATION psychology (variable reward, loot boxes, daily-login pressure).
  		features: [mastery_gated_progression, chapter_boss_set, retry_without_penalty, coop_study_room]

	5.5.9. 
	- id: coop_over_competition
  		label: Socialises through shared play, not public ranking
  		evidence: Gen Alpha socialising inside Roblox/Twitch; peer-collaborative routines
  		implication: >
    		Public leaderboards demotivate everyone outside the top decile and are the wrong incentive for a mastery subject. Use co-op and peer-explain instead.
  		features: [coop_study_room, peer_explain_pairing, private_progress_only]

	5.5.10. 
	- id: creator_identity
  		label: Wants to make and keep things, not just consume
  		evidence: Adobe 2025 (70% of 6–12s have used creative apps and value saving/sharing their work)
  		implication: >
    		Learner-generated artefacts outperform delivered content on both retention and attachment. Their mnemonic, their summary card, their explainer clip. Ownership is the retention mechanism.
  		features: [learner_authored_cards, mnemonic_cocreation, artefact_locker, shareable_summary]

	5.5.11. 
	- id: many_persistent_interests
  		label: Roughly twice as many identifiable interests as Gen Z, and they persist
  		evidence: Razorfish/GWI Gen Alpha study
  		implication: >
    		Strongest personalisation lever available that does not require behavioural profiling: let the learner DECLARE interests, then generate analogies and word problems from them. Declared > inferred (also the compliant route — see §5).
  		features: [declared_interest_profile, interest_anchored_analogy, contextual_word_problems]

	5.5.12. 
	- id: purpose_orientation
  		label: Needs to know why this matters
  		evidence: Deloitte 2026 (96% of Gen Z say purpose at work matters, up from 89%); 66% of Gen Alpha prefer purpose-driven companies
  		implication: >
    		Two distinct "why" answers per concept, both needed: exam utility ("this is worth 5 marks, appeared in 4 of the last 6 papers") and real utility ("this is how your phone's camera focuses"). Exam utility usually wins the session.
  		features: [why_this_matters, marks_weight_badge, real_world_hook]

	# ===== INDIA-SPECIFIC =====
	5.5.13. 
	- id: multilingual_code_switching
  		label: Mixes languages mid-sentence; technical terms stay English
  		evidence: Product requirement (English / Hindi / Kannada); standard Indian classroom register
  		implication: >
    		Accept Hinglish/Kanglish input without correction. Explain in the chosen language but PRESERVE the English technical vocabulary the board answer script requires. Budget for Indic script token inflation in context windows.
  		features: [code_switch_input, bilingual_term_pairing, script_aware_token_budget]

	5.5.14. 
	- id: board_exam_centrality
  		label: Board marks are the organising fact of the year
  		evidence: Indian board-exam structure; Deloitte 2026 on financial pressure shaping choices
  		implication: >
    		Every feature must answer "does this get me marks?" Honest marks-per-hour guidance beats breadth. Chapter weightage drives scheduling, not syllabus order.
  		features: [weightage_driven_scheduler, marking_scheme_feedback, marks_prediction]

	5.5.15. 
	- id: institutional_context
  		label: Reached through school/institution, used alongside a teacher
  		evidence: Product architecture (institution-first B2B2C)
  		implication: >
    		The teacher is a user. Learner-facing attention features need a teacher-facing counterpart, and this routing is also what the DPDP educational-institution exemption in §5 is scoped to.
  		features: [teacher_cohort_dashboard, misconception_rollup, parent_digest]
